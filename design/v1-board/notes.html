Decisions

The map is the surface; a picker chooses the gesture

A compact picker under the map chooses what the cursor does: Move, Draw, Arrow, Callout, Laser and Erase, each with a key and its own cursor. There are no property panels: every result is edited on the map.

Scenes are a film strip, not a menu

The strip is always on screen along the bottom, with a mini map of each scene and the game clock set large. Flipping is the main action, so it gets the most room and the arrow keys.

The frame says whether you're following

The brass map frame glows spirit-green while you present or follow, and is plain brass when you aren't. You can read your follow state without reading any text.

Presence sits on the scenes

Avatars ride on the scene card each person is looking at, and the presenter's card gets a dashed outline in their colour.

Anyone with edit rights can be followed

Several people can present at once. Owners and editors get a one-click Follow in the people list; view-only people can follow but can't be followed. The top bar names who you follow, and switching to another presenter is one click.

Following is one click either way

Everyone who isn't presenting sees the presenter's name and a Follow button in the top bar. Changing what you see (flipping, panning, zooming, switching floor) stops following, with a short notice and Follow again. There's no paused state.

Game clock is the scene's name

Scene titles start with the in-game time (“8:00 · mid boss setup”), and the clock is the typographic anchor in the strip and the title.

A link is a key, and editors can hand out keys

Owners and editors both mint view and edit links. The owner sees every link; editors see the ones they made and those shared from them. Revoking cascades, and the dialog counts what else gets cut off.

Honest save status

Saved, Syncing (n), Offline: n kept, Rejected. Offline work that can't merge becomes a named offline copy, never a silent drop.

Local art, schematic fallback

The real minimap, objective icons and hero portraits come from the git-ignored design/_art/, extracted from the local install. A fresh clone renders the original schematic, with no errors.

Federo for the clock, Atkinson for the rest

Federo, an art-deco face with a 1930s signage feel, sets clocks, titles and the wordmark. Atkinson Hyperlegible Next, designed for low-vision legibility, sets everything you read at a glance. Both are OFL and self-hosted.

Settled by the operator

Q1
Can the presenter pull people back?

Settled: no nudge. Following is easy instead, because the presenter's name and Follow are always in the top bar.

Q2
Does changing your view end follow, or pause it?

Settled: it ends it. Flipping, panning, zooming or switching floor stops following. Getting back is one click.

Q3
What does an edit-link holder see instead of Share?

Settled: editors get Share. They mint view and edit links and revoke their own. The owner sees all links, and revoking cascades.

Q4
Drag on empty map: arrow, or pan?

Settled by the tool picker: in Move, a drag on empty map pans, and arrows come from the Arrow tool.

Q5
Which typeface?

Settled: self-hosted OFL faces, Federo for display and Atkinson Hyperlegible Next for UI.

Still open

O1
Ink colour

Ink takes the drawer's presence colour here. Would you rather it take a team colour, or offer a small colour pick in the Draw tool?

O2
Laser for viewers

Laser travels as presence, and presence is the one thing a view link may send. Should view-link holders get Laser, so a caster or a sub can point too?

O3
Clearing ink

Erase works stroke by stroke. Is a “Clear ink on this scene” action worth a menu item, or is scrubbing enough?

O4
What a cut-off link holder sees

When a parent link is revoked, people on links shared from it see “This link no longer works”. Should it say which link was revoked, or just tell them to ask whoever shared it?
